Data Collection Facts Label

Data Source Facts Label
  1. Source Identity
Source name Youth Risk Behavior Survey (YRBS), 2023 National High School Dataset, part of CDC’s Youth Risk Behavior Surveillance System (YRBSS)
Domain/topic Public and adolescent health. It covers health behaviors, experiences, and conditions of high school students, including social media use frequency, bullying, mental health, sleep, and substance use.
Source type Government dataset. It is public, published by a U.S. federal agency, and downloadable with documentation.
  1. Collection Context
Collector/producer CDC’s Division of Adolescent and School Health (National Center for Chronic Disease Prevention and Health Promotion), a public federal agency. ICF served as the survey contractor. The CDC and ICF institutional review boards approved the protocol.
Original purpose The YRBSS started in 1991 to keep track of health risk behaviors in U.S. high school students. The CDC wanted to see how common these behaviors are, how they change over time, and how they overlap, plus keep an eye on Healthy People goals. It also grew to track experiences and conditions that affect health. In 2023 the CDC added new questions, social media included, to get national data on newer topics.
Collection method Students took the survey on tablets during one class period. It was anonymous and voluntary. The tablets weren’t connected to the internet, so data collectors synced everything to a central spot at the end of each day. Students who were absent could take a web version when they came back (323 did). Local parent permission rules were followed, and it was offered in English and Spanish. Schools were picked with a three-stage cluster sample: counties first, then schools, then classrooms.
Record-creation event A student in a picked classroom finishes the questionnaire. The record stays in the data if it passes quality control.
  1. Observation and Scope
Unit of observation One student. One finished questionnaire is one row.
Population or objects represented Students in grades 9 to 12 at public (charter included) and private schools in the 50 states and D.C. Left out of the school list: alternative schools, special education schools, Department of Defense schools, Bureau of Indian Education schools, vocational schools where students also attend another school, and schools with 40 or fewer students in grades 9 to 12. The CDC says homeschooled students and students not in school aren’t represented. In 2023 they added an extra American Indian/Alaska Native sample, and Black and Hispanic students were oversampled.
Time period covered Spring semester (January to June) 2023. The survey happens every two years, and 2023 was the first year with a social media question.
Geographic area coverage The whole U.S. (50 states and D.C.). 155 schools took part. The data is weighted so it represents U.S. high schoolers overall.
  1. Variables and Data Structure
Main variables/fields Age (Q1, 12 or younger up to 18 or older), sex, grade, race and ethnicity, self-reported height and weight, BMI, how often they use social media (Q80, 8 options from not at all to more than once an hour), bullied at school (Q24), electronically bullied (Q25), sad or hopeless (Q26), suicide questions (Q27 to Q29), poor mental health (Q84), sleep (Q85), grades (Q87), questions on tobacco, alcohol, drugs, sex, diet, and exercise, a yes/no version of every question (QN#), and the survey design variables WEIGHT, STRATUM, and PSU.
Data types included Mostly categorical. Multiple choice answers are saved as codes, and a lot of them are frequency scales. Race (Q5) lets you pick more than one. Height and weight were typed in, then converted to meters and kilograms. BMI and BMI percentile are calculated numbers. WEIGHT is numeric, PSU and STRATUM are design codes, and the QN# variables are 1 for yes and 2 for no. I didn’t find any free text, images, GPS, audio, or sensor data in the documentation.
Direct measurements Everything is a survey answer with no sensors or physical measurements, so the directly recorded items are things students reported like age, sex, grade, height and weight, how often they use social media, yes/no on bullying, and hours of sleep on a school night.
Possible proxy variables The CDC doesn’t label any variable a proxy, but ones to look at in the next phase are Q80 for overall social media use (it doesn’t ask which app, what they do on it, or how long), Q26 and Q84 for depression, anxiety, or wellbeing, Q25 for online harassment, and the BMI, overweight, and obese flags that are calculated from self-reported height and weight.
  1. Documentation
Documentation found The 2023 YRBS Data User’s Guide with the codebook (https://www.cdc.gov/yrbs/media/pdf/2023/2023_National_YRBS_Data_Users_Guide508.pdf), the CDC methods report by Brener et al. (https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a1.htm), the CDC social media report by Young et al. (https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a3.htm), the 2023 questionnaire (https://www.cdc.gov/yrbs/questionnaires/index.html), SAS and SPSS programs for reading the data (https://www.cdc.gov/yrbs/data/index.html), and the online tools Youth Online (https://nccd.cdc.gov/Youthonline/App/Default.aspx), YRBS Analysis Tool (https://yrbs-analysis.cdc.gov), and YRBS Explorer (https://yrbs-explorer.services.cdc.gov).
Stated collection details 20,386 questionnaires were finished in 155 schools and 20,103 passed quality control, with response rates of 49.8% for schools, 71.0% for students, and 35.4% overall, missing data wasn’t filled in with estimates, the weights adjust for nonresponse and oversampling, not every student got every question (so how many answered each one changes, and 4,900 are missing on the social media question), the CDC cites a test-retest study showing most questions were reliable, it’s a cross-sectional survey, and the CDC doesn’t state why individual schools or students declined.
